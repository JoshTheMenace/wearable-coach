# CPR lesson storyboard

Proposed design, September 16, 2026. This is a content and interaction plan, not a change to the running lesson. It follows the existing adult, compression-only manikin scope. Target presentation length: roughly four to five minutes, allowing for questions and one replay. The learner presses Start on the phone once; everything below happens through the glasses and voice.

## Recommendation

Make this a small authored course with a conversational instructor: a brief opening, two teaching pages, the existing demonstration, coached practice, and a personal recap. Give the teaching pages real explanatory content and clear visual hierarchy. Reduce the display to a single useful cue once hands-on work starts.

Use **two visual sections within one stable page**, rather than two independently selectable cards. The first section carries the concept; the second explains it or gives the next action. Voice advances the page. No required scrolling, small bottom controls, or “More on phone.”

The opening should take about a minute. The video then makes the concepts concrete. A question or request for more detail can extend any page; the default path should keep moving at the learner's pace.

## Visual language

- A small chapter label, a clear sentence-case heading, and a short paragraph. Start with about 20–35 displayed words on a reading page; validate wrapping and legibility on the actual display.
- A stable upper-middle composition inside an explicit 600×600 canvas. Keep essential content away from the bottom region the wearer found difficult to read. The proposed inset is a starting point, not a measured universal safe area.
- Bright, large text; restrained separators; one accent used for the current task. Do not rely on the perceived opacity of a filled panel or on color alone.
- Use a short paragraph when explaining one idea. Use two stacked sections when contrasting a target and an action. Avoid two columns of small text.
- A diagram earns space by showing the hand target more clearly than another sentence. Put captions in native text, not tiny labels baked into an image.
- Keep the course heading and voice hint in consistent positions. The hint belongs directly below the content, not at the bottom edge.
- During practice, show a compact stage indicator and one instruction. Reuse the richer layout for a requested explanation or recap.

### Three presentation templates

1. **Teach:** chapter label → heading → explanatory paragraph → one short supporting section → voice hint.
2. **Show:** title and optional diagram, or the video occupying the display. Video and coach speech take turns.
3. **Practice:** one active cue → a small, evidence-based status → replay/pause hint. A correction replaces the cue; it does not add another panel.

## Exact proposed sequence

The spoken lines below are authored defaults. The coach can answer questions naturally, but it should preserve the facts, the current step, and the distinction between observation and measurement.

### 1. Opening: make the task clear

**On glasses**

> ADULT CPR · MANIKIN PRACTICE\
> **Compression skills**\
> Learn hand placement. Watch the demo. Practise with live coaching.\
> **Our scenario**\
> Adult collapse. Unresponsive, with absent or abnormal breathing.\
> Say “Next”

**Coach**

“Today we'll practise adult chest compressions on your manikin. In our scenario, help has been called and someone is getting an AED. We'll learn the technique, watch it, then try it together. This is a practice exercise; don't make a real emergency call. Say ‘next’ when you're ready.”

**Transition:** wait for Next or a natural equivalent. A question holds this page. Say the introduction once per lesson attempt, never again because a connection recovered.

**Design purpose:** establish a course and a concrete task without making the wearer navigate a menu. The full emergency response sequence is outside this short compression practice; questions can retrieve the scoped reference facts.

### 2. Teach hand placement

**On glasses**

> LEARN · 1 OF 2\
> **Find the hand position**\
> Place the heel of one hand on the lower half of the breastbone, in the centre of the chest. Stack your other hand above it.\
> Say “Next”

**Optional illustration variant:** show a simple chest/manikin diagram and shorten the paragraph to “Heel of one hand on the lower breastbone. Other hand on top.” The longer explanation remains available through “Explain that.” Do not squeeze both full text and a large diagram into the same page.

**Coach**

“The contact point is the heel of your lower hand. Your other hand stacks above it. The illustration will show the breastbone target. In a moment, the video will show this from another angle.”

Until a diagram is supplied, replace the illustration sentence with “Watch for that contact point in the video.”

**Transition:** Next opens the second teaching page. Back returns to the opening. Repeat replays only this explanation. If the learner asks to see the demonstration here, honor that request instead of imposing a navigation detour; retain a short technique reminder before practice.

### 3. Teach the compression pattern

**On glasses**

> LEARN · 2 OF 2\
> **Press, release, repeat**\
> **100–120 / min** · Compression target\
> Press down, then release your weight so the chest can rise fully before the next compression.\
> Say “Show the demo”

This is the best first use of the two-section layout: a prominent numerical target above a readable explanation. It supplies more substance without a wall of text.

**Coach**

“Keep your shoulders above your hands and your arms straight. For an average adult, compress at least five centimetres, avoiding more than six, and allow full recoil. Those are technique targets; I can't measure depth or force from these glasses. Say ‘show the demo’ to watch.”

**Transition:** Show the demo requests the existing overview. Next can mean the same thing on this page. More detail remains on demand. Do not introduce a metronome or display a live cadence measurement in this version.

### 4. Watch the demonstration

**Before playback**

> WATCH · DEMONSTRATION\
> **Watch the hand contact point**\
> Preparing your video…

**Coach:** “Watch where the heel of the lower hand rests, and how the chest is allowed to rise.”

**While playing:** the existing video occupies the display. The coach stays silent; the clip's audio plays through the glasses. No lesson text competes with the footage.

Use the prepared **55-second overview**, source **00:50–01:45**. Playback completion advances to the rehearsal prompt. A request being accepted is not proof the video started; the preparing state remains until the native player reports playing.

**Voice controls:** Pause the video, replay the video, skip the demonstration, end the session. Until seek/resume position is implemented and verified, a paused video's continuation must say “I'll restart this clip,” rather than claiming to resume from its old timestamp.

**Failure:** keep the watch step and say, once, “The video didn't start. Say ‘try again’ or ‘skip the demonstration.’” Skipping this video does not skip hand-placement verification.

### 5. Rehearse the starting position

**On glasses**

> PRACTISE · STARTING POSITION\
> **Show your starting position**\
> Place both hands on the manikin. Hold before beginning compressions.\
> Say “Ready”

**Coach:** “Your turn. Set your hands on the manikin and hold there. Say ‘ready’ when you'd like to begin.”

Ready arms practice, rather than declaring placement correct. Fresh, clear placement observations determine whether to begin, correct the position, or request a clearer view. Two consistent observations are the existing prototype's guard, not clinical validation.

**If clearly positioned:** “Your hands appear to be on the target area. Begin when you're ready.”

**If unclear:** “I can't see the hand contact point clearly. Show me both hands and the manikin's chest.” Do not ask the wearer to reach for the phone, guess their posture, or announce success.

**Transition:** start the short practice when the learner is ready and placement is accepted. An explicit request to continue without a visual check proceeds with “Placement not verified” retained in the record. The display distinguishes this fallback from observed placement.

### 6. Coached practice and correction

**Normal display**

> PRACTISE · COMPRESSIONS\
> **Keep the chest in view**\
> Aim for 100–120 compressions/min\
> Say “Pause” or “Show placement again”

**Coach at entry:** “Practise for a short round. I'll watch the hand position and speak if I see something to adjust.” Then allow silence.

For the planned presentation, deliberately move the hands too low only on the manikin. A correction must come from the actual observer result, never from elapsed time or a scripted demo trigger.

**When the evidence supports too-low placement**

> ADJUST HAND POSITION\
> **Move your hands slightly higher**\
> Return the heel of your lower hand to the breastbone target.\
> Say “Show placement again”

**Coach:** “Pause your practice. Your hands appear below the target area. Move them slightly higher onto the breastbone.”

Do not claim “on the belly” unless the observation supports that more specific statement. Do not give a measured distance or say to move to the upper chest. Keep the cue on screen until fresh evidence shows the adjustment; suppress repeated identical spoken corrections while the learner responds.

Detection is asynchronous. The desired experience is a prompt interruption, but present latency must be measured, not advertised as a guaranteed one-second response. A stale or uncertain view cannot trigger a confident correction.

### 7. Requested micro-replay

**Learner:** “Can I see the hand placement again?”

**Coach:** “Yes. Pause your practice and watch this close-up.”

**On glasses before playback:** “HAND PLACEMENT · 5-second replay” and “Preparing your video…”

Play the prepared **five-second excerpt**, source **01:01.5–01:06.5**, with the coach silent. Camera observation pauses while the demonstration owns the display. The excerpt is a teaching asset, never evidence of what the learner did.

**After the player ends:** restore the previous practice or correction state, then say: “Now set your hands on the manikin again. I'll check the position before you continue.”

Obtain fresh observations. Do not restore a green check from before the replay, restart the lesson, replay the overview, or immediately repeat the earlier correction from stale evidence.

### 8. Corrected attempt and recap

**After a supported correction:** “Your hands now appear on the target area. Try another short round.” If that finding is unavailable, keep the verification pending.

Once the learner has had another opportunity to practise, say “When you've finished this practice round, say ‘I'm done.’” A suggested 20–30-second rehearsal is a presentation choice, not a prescribed CPR stopping point. It should never count as proof that compressions occurred.

**On glasses after explicit completion, if these events actually occurred**

> PRACTICE RECAP\
> **You adjusted and tried again**\
> Demonstration watched\
> Hand position adjusted · visually observed\
> Practice finished · you confirmed\
> Depth and force were not measured

**Coach:** “You reviewed the demonstration and adjusted your hand position after feedback. You've finished this practice round. I haven't measured compression depth or force. Say ‘practise again,’ ‘review placement,’ or ‘end session.’”

Generate the recap from recorded events: use “Demonstration skipped,” “Placement not verified,” or “No adjustment needed” when appropriate. Never invent a correction to make the recap more impressive. If six short lines are too much on the device, make a second recap page rather than shrinking text.

## Transition and conversation rules

| Learner intent | Behaviour |
| --- | --- |
| Next / Back | Navigate teaching pages; Next on the final teaching page requests the overview. During practice, Next does not certify or skip an observed skill. |
| Repeat / Explain that | Keep the current screen and repeat or expand its explanation. |
| A question | Hold progression, retrieve scoped reference facts when needed, answer briefly, and return to the same task with one continuation cue. Avoid a full lesson restart. |
| Pause | Hold the current lesson state and stop coach speech. Keep listening for Resume. During playback, accept explicit “Pause the video.” |
| Show placement again | Play the named excerpt, preserving where the learner was and invalidating old placement evidence. |
| Skip the demonstration | Skip viewing only; proceed to placement rehearsal. |
| Continue without camera | Explicitly bypass visual verification; preserve its unverified status. Never use this phrase as the video-failure remedy. |
| I'm done | Finish an active practice round, then recap its actual events. |
| End session | Stop the session, camera, microphone, and playback. |

- Advance teaching pages on the learner's intent, not a reading timeout. Playback completion is the deliberate automatic transition. A correction is the deliberate interruption.
- Display each page before its spoken explanation. A question can interrupt narration. A next-page request cancels old narration before the new page starts speaking.
- Recover to the same page and progress. A connection problem is not a new lesson. Announce a persistent interruption briefly; do not narrate every transient state change.
- Use voice as the required control path. Native selection can be an optional equivalent after testing; no dependence on custom swipe gestures or phone controls.
- Sources belong in lesson metadata and an on-demand “Where does that come from?” response. Do not read a citation before every instruction.

## Diagram brief

One simple adult manikin/chest illustration, viewed from the learner's side or above, with the breastbone and lower-hand heel clearly distinguished. Use one restrained highlighted target and minimal labels. A second image may show a too-low position and the correction direction, but its arrow must terminate at the same anatomical target used by the reference and observer.

Create an instructional asset, not a photorealistic decorative image. Have the anatomy and target checked before treating it as an authoritative teaching reference. Do not place tiny labels within the image. Include a text-only fallback so the course does not depend on this asset being ready.

## Implementation plan after runtime fixes

1. **Author the content.** Store stable page IDs, paragraph blocks, spoken defaults, source fact IDs, optional image IDs, and allowed transitions. Keep learning pages separate from the compact practice HUD. Review the selected overview and excerpt with their actual audio to confirm continuity and scope.
2. **Add native lesson templates.** Reuse the explicit 600×600 root and the lab's successful image/native-text path. Keep one focus surface. Validate text wrapping before submission; split overflow into an on-glasses page. Retain the lab's 15 KB compressed-layout guard for illustrated layouts.
3. **Keep progress server-owned.** Separate the current teaching page from the current practice phase. Voice tools express intents; they cannot invent checks or arbitrarily replace the authored screen. Stable narration IDs prevent reconnects from repeating an opening.
4. **Serialize media transitions.** Finish the cue, hand display/audio to the movie, pause observation, and restore the saved lesson step when the player completes, fails, or is stopped. Reacquire fresh placement evidence before resuming correction feedback.
5. **Ground questions.** Reuse the existing lookup tool and fact IDs. A question may open one short explanation page, then return to the prior lesson page. It must not silently change the curriculum or the verification record.
6. **Rehearse the real presentation.** Start once on phone; complete teaching, overview, clear/unclear placement, actual too-low correction, five-second replay, corrected attempt, recap, and spoken end without touching it. Repeat after a transient reconnect.

Record page visibility acknowledgements separately from wearer-confirmed legibility; narration start/stop and duplicates; transition intents; video request/playing/ended times; observer evidence age and correction timing; and whether practice resumes on the right page. No fabricated accuracy score or pass/fail certification.

## Evidence and open questions

The lab established native diagrams, lesson pages, and selections on DAT 0.8. The production renderer still flattens content into a short generic HUD and does not yet integrate that image path. Full layout replacement also reset focus/scroll in the lab. Those findings support a small native page renderer with voice navigation, not a new web runtime or a scroll-heavy course player. See [display lab results](display-lab-hardware-results.md) and [current prototype](cpr-demo-prototype.md).

Paragraph density and the exact two-section composition are proposed, not wearer-validated. Test the densest teaching page first at a readable font size. Reduce content per page before reducing the font. Optical visibility cannot be inferred from the desktop storyboard.

Fable's review usefully challenged the amount of pre-video explanation and unmeasured numbers on the practice display. This plan keeps two focused teaching pages plus a short opening, makes depth a spoken teaching target, and uses evidence-based recap wording. It does not adopt unsupported anatomical claims, a fixed four-line ceiling, or a rigid restriction on when the learner may request the demonstration.

## Clinical grounding

Clinical statements use the supplied seed facts, checked against [AHA 2025 adult basic life support](https://cpr.heart.org/en/resuscitation-science/cpr-and-ecc-guidelines/adult-basic-life-support) and [Red Cross adult CPR instructions](https://www.redcross.org/take-a-class/cpr/performing-cpr/cpr-steps). These cover hand position, cadence, depth, recoil, and emergency-response context. The short lesson stays within adult manikin compression practice.

[AHA resuscitation education guidance](https://cpr.heart.org/en/resuscitation-science/cpr-and-ecc-guidelines/resuscitation-education-science) supports feedback devices and notes limits of visual assessment. It does not validate this prototype's observer. The recap must distinguish visible hand placement from unmeasured compression quality; this demo is not a complete CPR course or certification.
