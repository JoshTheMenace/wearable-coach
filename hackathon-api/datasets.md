## 350: Educational Wargaming in Higher Education: A Review of Empirical Studies

Here's a description you can use for the hackathon dataset listing:

---

**Educational Wargaming in Higher Education: A Review of Empirical Studies**
Bennerstedt, U., & Sjöblom, B. (2026). *Scandinavian Journal of Military Studies*, 9(1), 240–260. DOI: [10.31374/sjms.451](https://doi.org/10.31374/sjms.451) · Open access (CC-BY 4.0)

This peer-reviewed article is a semi-systematic review of 15 empirical studies on educational wargaming in higher education and professional military education, published between 2014 and 2024. Rather than just cataloguing findings, the authors build a conceptual framework — grounded in the Presage–Process–Product (3P) model — for analyzing *how* learning is defined, measured, and theoretically justified across different wargaming approaches.

**Why it matters for this hackathon category:** the paper's central finding is that most existing wargaming programs struggle to answer a basic question — is learning actually happening, and how would you know? It documents recurring gaps that map directly onto solvable product problems:
- Inconsistent or weak operationalization of "learning" as a measurable construct
- Limited attention to facilitation and debrief quality as drivers of learning outcomes
- Little longitudinal tracking of whether wargaming-derived skills persist or transfer
- Loose integration between learning theory and wargame design/assessment practice

For teams building in the Wargaming in Training and Education space, this article offers a research-backed problem statement, a ready-made assessment framework (3P) to build against, and a literature map of adjacent theory (Ellström, Boyd's OODA, Kolb, Bloom's taxonomy, etc.) that can ground a solution's design rationale rather than starting from scratch.

## 349: OUTCOMES-BASED MILITARY EDUCATION PROCEDURES FOR OFFICER JOINT PROFESSIONAL MILITARY EDUCATION

📌 Context & Overview
CJCSM 1810.01A (Outcomes-Based Military Education Procedures for Officer Joint Professional Military Education, dated 12 February 2026) is the authoritative Joint Staff manual governing how Officer Joint Professional Military Education (JPME) programs design, measure, accredit, and continuously improve officer education.
For hackathon teams, this manual serves as the regulatory baseline and evaluation ontology for any AI tool touching curriculum design, learning analytics, student assessment, or automated feedback systems.
________________________________________
🎯 Document Significance 
Focus Area	Core Takeaway	Hackathon Application
Paradigm Shift	Replaces legacy "seat-time/topics taught" metrics with Outcomes-Based Military Education (OBME).	AI tools should focus on measuring demonstrated capability and performance outcomes rather than simple completion logs.
Data & Governance	Integrates accreditation into the Joint Accreditation Management Information System (JAMIS) via Annual Common Educational Standards (CES) and Biennial PLO Reports.	Dashboard and workflow prototypes can target streamlining or automating JAMIS data ingest, rubric calibration, and reporting.
Quality Standards	Defines six Common Educational Standards (CES) (Joint Acculturation, Academic Experience, Student Achievement, Program Review, Faculty Assessment, and Infrastructure).	Intelligent tutors or analytics platforms must align with and preserve standards such as CES 1 (Joint Acculturation) and CES 3 (Student Achievement).
________________________________________
💡 Hackathon Use Case Alignment: Leveraging JLA Capabilities
When developing hackathon use cases involving Learner Analysis, Performance Evaluation, or Adaptive Learning Engines, your solutions should map directly to the Joint Learning Areas (JLAs) defined in CJCSM 1810.01A.
1. Learner Analysis & Student Profiling
•	JLA & DLA Mapping: Use AI to tag student writing, war-gaming decisions, or discussion contributions against Joint Learning Areas (JLAs) and Desired Leader Attributes (DLAs) such as strategic thinking, joint operational art, and cross-domain synergy.
•	Gaps Analysis: Track individual officer mastery trajectories across joint competencies over time, providing personalized recommendations to bridge capability gaps before deployment or staff assignments.
2. Performance Evaluation & Direct Assessment (CES 3 Alignment)
•	Automated Rubric Evaluation: Build AI scoring models tuned to authentic joint staff deliverables (e.g., Commander’s Estimates, Joint Operation Plans, Concept of Operations).
•	Formative Feedback Assistants: Provide real-time, objective feedback to students during exercises, evaluating how well their decisions reflect high-level joint doctrine and strategic intent.
•	Faculty Assistance Tools: Reduce grading burdens by pre-evaluating student assignments against PLO rubrics, allowing faculty to focus on high-touch mentoring and Joint Acculturation (CES 1).
