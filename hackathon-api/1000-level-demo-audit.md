## How to read the verdicts

- **Practical:** ordinary props support a credible solo part-task now. This does not mean it is the strongest presentation.
- **Conditional:** potentially useful, but a loan, suitable reference, extra camera or expert reviewer is needed. No loan is confirmed.
- **Analogue:** accessible equipment can demonstrate the coaching capability, but it does not reproduce the military event.
- **Weak fit:** a fragment or tabletop/voice version is feasible, but it misses your preference for physical, first-person coaching.
- **Exclude:** the actual task needs a unit, field setting, specialist system, controlled range or other resources outside this brief. Exclusion is for this demo, not a judgment about the value of the training.

None of the stage adaptations establishes military qualification. A successful AI demo is evidence that the prototype can observe and coach the chosen prop task; transfer to military equipment would need separate evaluation.

### Recommended presentation structure

Choose one short physical task, not a tour of many tasks. Preassemble everything except the few steps being taught. Show an actual visible mistake or an uncertain decision; have the coach ask for a better view if the camera cannot resolve it. The learner corrects the problem and performs a short second attempt without the same hint. The spectator display shows the supporting frame, brief transcript, checkpoint and evidence-based review. A visible working result is stronger than an unsupported numerical score.

For the Cyborg Hand, prepare most of the build beforehand and select one manufacturer-documented finger/linkage subassembly or fit/adjustment check. Demonstrate closing the hand around a light object afterward. Test the exact task and camera view before committing; there are over 200 parts, so a complete build is too much for a short stage demo.
