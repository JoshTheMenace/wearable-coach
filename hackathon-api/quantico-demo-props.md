## Which I would use

For a blaster-shaped prop, the **Arctic Zerostriker** is the concrete nearby choice. Its attachment-only assembly may be too shallow for the strongest coaching demonstration.

For actual mechanical learning, I would choose the **Cyborg Hand**, prepare most of it offstage, and test a short manufacturer-documented subassembly that is easy to see from the glasses. End with the hand grasping a light object. The coach should detect or ask about a visible mismatch, explain a relevant diagram, then reduce assistance on the next attempt. Use the product manual as the procedure source; this is a general maintenance-training analogue, not military weapon instruction.

For the strongest direct connection to the infantry manual, investigate a same-day loan of a medical limb trainer and training tourniquet from the organizers, with an instructor providing the exercise and rubric. Availability is **unconfirmed**. A consumer-network troubleshooting exercise is the strongest option using equipment your team may already have.
